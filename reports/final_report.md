# marble-home: final technical report

Every number below is produced by a script from files under `results/` (tables in `results_tables.md`, generated by
`scripts/make_report.py`). Where a claim rests on a single seed or an assumption, it says so. Read `docs/limitations.md` alongside this.

## 1. What this is

A reproducible stack in which a simulated **Fourier GR1 humanoid** (fixed base, "Level A") performs table-top cup tasks in procedural
and **real World Labs Marble** rooms, using MuJoCo 3.3.7 + RoboSuite 1.5.2 for physics and PyTorch / Stable-Baselines3 for learning.
Hardware: Windows 11, i5-8250U, 16 GB, **no GPU** (Profile A). All timing numbers were measured on a shared CPU and are contended lower bounds.

## 3. Accuracy improvement (the main late finding)

Initial results (controller **v1**, RoboSuite defaults) had a thin placement margin (3.3-4.0 cm against a 4.5 cm tolerance), a weak reach
policy (70 %), and a scripted expert that failed deterministically in the far corners of the cup-spawn region (81 % on a 21-point grid).
Diagnosis and fix, each measured:

1. **Cause (expert failures):** the whole-body IK pins the waist (posture weight 10), so far-outboard / far-forward grasps were unreachable.
   Scan: weight 10 -> 33 % on the failing corners; weight <= 5 -> 100 %, hand error 0.7 cm (`waist_weight_scan.json`). **v2 sets weight 1.**
2. **Side effect found:** the waist then swings up to ~0.68 rad (39 deg) when reaching, a large whole-body lean (visible in the place video).
3. **Absolute-target BC collapsed under v2** (13.3 % on pick) while the same demos relabelled as **delta actions** gave 100 %. Explanation
   supported by a step-by-step trace: the expert commands a target that leads the hand by 3-5 cm; a policy mapping measured pose -> absolute
   target regresses toward "pose + small lead" and creeps. Delta actions remove the dependence on tracking lag. (Mechanism is a hypothesis
   consistent with the trace and the fix, not a controlled ablation.)
4. **Stricter success rules** adopted so "accuracy" means something: reach tolerance 5 -> 3 cm with a 5-step hold; place radius 4.5 -> 3 cm.

Result (same 30 held-out seeds; see the generated table for the v1 / v2 columns and confidence intervals): expert 100 % on pick, place
and reach; BC-MLP pick 100 %, place 100 % (cup error 0.70 cm mean, 2.22 cm worst), reach 100 %; hand error 0.5-1.8 cm. **These v2 numbers
are under stricter rules for reach and place, so the improvement is larger than the raw percentages suggest.**
All of these are at the ceiling of what 30 episodes can resolve (lower Wilson bound 88.6 %).

## 4. Policy comparison (pick_up_cup, v2, state observations, 30 held-out instances)

| policy | success | note |
|---|---|---|
| random | 0 % | |
| scripted expert | 100 % | |
| BC-MLP, delta actions (240 demos) | 100 % | best |
| BC-MLP, absolute-target actions | 13.3 % | fails under v2 (section 3) |
| diffusion policy, delta actions | 73.3 % [55.6, 85.8] | worse than BC; reproduced in two evaluations |

**BC beat diffusion here.** The demonstrations are unimodal scripted trajectories, so diffusion has no multimodality to exploit, and its
imprecise reaches (and 8-step open-loop chunks) cost accuracy. The v1 diffusion sweep showed 1-2 DDIM steps give 0 %, 5 steps 83 %, 20 steps
90 % (v1 controller; same-checkpoint DDIM-10 scored 76.7 % and 86.7 % in two runs, so differences under ~10 points are noise). **Inference
latency was not measured reliably** (CPU contention), so no latency claim is made.

**PPO (reach_cup):** v1, 24.6k steps, deterministic held-out: delta actions 40-50 %; absolute targets 0-7 %; BC-initialised actor 0 %
(diagnostic: the distilled actor already failed before any PPO update, 1/10, despite 0.0005 imitation MSE). **Under v2 (stricter reach rule)
PPO delta scored 0/20.** PPO from scratch at this budget does not meet the tolerance; no claim is made that RL beats imitation here.

## 4b. Multi-item household tasks

Rigid-body proxies on the same stack (details and caveats in `simulation/layouts.py`, `docs/limitations.md` item 8): **clear_table** (3 cups to a
flat tray), **laundry_basket** (3 cylindrical "laundry rolls" to a tote), **load_dishwasher** (2 cups to the rack plate of a static counter-top
dishwasher; no door or sliding). An item counts when it is on its slot (3 cm, upright, settled, hand released) and it must still be there at the end;
<= 5 invalid contact steps overall. State observations, delta actions, 100 scripted demos per task, held-out seeds 900000+, 30 episodes per row.

| task | scripted expert | BC-MLP seeds 0 / 1 / 2 | pooled BC (n=90) |
|---|---|---|---|
| clear_table | 93.3 % | 63.3 / 76.7 / 93.3 % | 77.8 % [68.2, 85.1] |
| laundry_basket | 100 % | 96.7 / 83.3 / 90.0 % | 90.0 % [82.1, 94.6] |
| load_dishwasher | 100 % | 96.7 / 93.3 / 96.7 % | 95.6 % [89.1, 98.3] |

* The three-seed spread on clear_table (63 to 93 %) is as large as the gap to the expert: with one seed the conclusion would have been either
  "fine" or "poor". Seeds differ in training only; the demos and test instances are identical.
* The layouts were **engineered until the scripted expert passed**. Two things were physically required and are baked into them: items spawn in
  y-bands that increase with x (the open hand reaches ~0.2 m ahead of the grasp site and a neighbour in that corridor is knocked over; first
  version 17 % expert success, then 92 to 100 %), and slots are >= 11 cm apart and filled far-to-near (the wrist trails the item toward -x).
* The dishwasher policy trained at 0.85 m failed 4 of 4 renders in a procedural kitchen with a different counter height; the numbers above
  hold only for the training geometry (also the geometry of all Marble rooms here).

### 4c. Counter-height generalisation (load_dishwasher, `scripts/run_multiheight.py`; one seed, 15 held-out episodes per height)
The scripted expert solves the task at every height probed (0.79-0.94 m, 8 episodes each, 100 %), so the failure is in the learned policy.

| policy | training heights 0.82/0.85/0.88/0.91 m | held-out heights 0.835/0.865/0.90 m |
|---|---|---|
| BC trained at 0.85 m only (100 demos) | 25.0 % (n=60) [15.8, 37.2]; per height 0 / 100 / 0 / 0 % | 2.2 % (n=45) [0.4, 11.6] |
| BC trained on 4 heights (30 demos each) | 81.7 % (n=60) [70.1, 89.4]; 60 / 87 / 87 / 93 % | 88.9 % (n=45) [76.5, 95.2]; 87 / 100 / 80 % |

Training across heights removes the collapse inside the trained span. Outside it the policy does not extrapolate (same checkpoint, 15 episodes
each): 0.79 m (3 cm below the span) 0.0 % [0, 20.4], 0.94 m (3 cm above) 26.7 % [10.9, 52.0]. Caveats: the held-out heights inside the table lie
within the trained span (interpolation), a single training seed was used, the 0.82 m cell is the weakest (60 %), and only the dishwasher task was retrained.
* A bug found on the way: a placed item that had just been released counted as a "scene collision" while the hand retreated; contacts with the
  active item are manipulation, contacts with other items are collisions. Both rules are in `kitchen_env._contact_stats`.

## 5. Generalisation

* **Scene level** (10 train / 3 val / 5 held-out test procedural scenes, counter height, depth, furniture, colours vary): see the generated table.
  v2: multi-scene BC 100 % / 97 % / **80 %** (train / val / test), a real gap (intervals do not overlap); single-scene BC 10 % / 0 % / 0 %;
  expert 100 % everywhere. Almost all of the gap came from one held-out scene.
* **Fix:** removing the two placement-goal inputs (irrelevant for pick, but varying per scene) gave **100 % / 100 % / 100 % (50/50 on test)**.
  One seed, 50 test episodes; applies only where the goal is irrelevant. Consistent with a spurious-feature explanation; not a controlled ablation.
* **Marble:** the trained multi-scene BC picked the cup inside all five real Marble rooms (videos 08_*). Because the counter, robot and cup are
  composed identically in each room, the manipulation metrics are identical across them; Marble contributes static surroundings, not task difficulty.

## 6. Marble integration: what is and is not established

* 5 real worlds (1 per category), `marble-1.0-draft`, 230 credits each = 1,150 credits. Collision meshes imported and validated (compile, step,
  collision-free spawn, expert matches the open kitchen).
* **Every scale is an assumption** (API returned `semantics_metadata: null`): footprint-area matching for 4 rooms; the laundry room only fits at
  scale >= 1.2 (44 % larger than prompted). Two rooms get implausibly low ceilings. Only the draft model was tried.
* Real meshes exposed two pipeline bugs that the synthetic fixture could not (box budget too small; **closed room shells were voxel-filled solid**);
  both fixed with regression tests.
* Splats were downloaded and checksummed but never rendered. No policy was *trained* in a Marble scene.

## 7. What was NOT done (and why)

* **Re-run on controller v2, delta actions (pick_up_cup, 30 held-out episodes, tables in `results_tables.md`):**
  * *Data scaling (BC-MLP, 3 seeds):* 10 demos 27/20/40 %, 25: 80/93/43 %, 50: 97/100/83 %, 100: 100/100/100 %, 240: 100/97/80 %. v2 reaches ~100 %
    at 100 demos (v1 needed 240); no gain at 240; validation MSE falls monotonically (0.037 to 0.020) but does not predict which seeds fail.
  * *DDIM steps (one diffusion checkpoint):* 1 step 0 %, 2 steps 0 %, 5 steps 83 %, 10 steps 73 %, 20 steps 63 %. 5/10/20 are within noise; unlike v1,
    more steps no longer help.
  * *Proprio-only control (no cup information):* 27 / 23 / 17 % over 3 seeds.
  * *Image policy (CNN-BC, 64x64 RGB + proprio, 150 demos):* 13-23 % in the four train/test visual-randomisation cells, overlapping the proprio-only
    control's 17-27 % (two cells sit slightly below it): **no evidence the CNN uses pixels beyond what a blind policy achieves**. The first evaluation reported 0 % everywhere because
    `train_bc_images` did not save `action_mode` in the checkpoint, so delta-trained CNNs were evaluated with absolute actions; fixed in the trainer,
    regression test added, checkpoints patched and re-evaluated. Only 30 episodes per cell.
  * *Curriculum (PPO, reach_cup, 30k steps, 2 seeds per arm):* fixed / manual / adaptive all 0 to 3 %. PPO does not solve the stricter reach task
    at this budget, so no curriculum effect can be measured.
  * *Inference latency (policy only, 1 thread, CPU shared with other jobs, min over repeats):* BC-MLP 0.45 ms; diffusion per 8-step plan DDIM-1 9.1,
    DDIM-2 15.7, DDIM-5 41.8, DDIM-10 84.7, DDIM-20 174.8 ms (1.1 to 21.9 ms per control step). CPU only; no GPU claim.
* **Domain-randomisation grid on v2: INCOMPLETE.** Datasets were collected and prepared for all four train conditions, but only the first of 32
  planned evaluations finished (train none / test none: 100 %) before I stopped the run, which had spent 1.7 CPU-hours inside a single evaluation
  without finishing it (cause not diagnosed; randomised physics may make episodes very slow). No DR conclusion is drawn.
* Joint-position / operational-space action variants; hand-orientation and bimanual learning; any real-robot transfer.

## 8. Recommended next experiments

1. Diagnose and finish the v2 domain-randomisation grid (the physics-randomised evaluation hangs or is extremely slow); train the household policies
   across counter heights and scenes, and with image or noisy-pose observations, since the present results use privileged item poses.
2. Train and evaluate policies *inside* the Marble scenes with the true scale (needs `metric_scale_factor`, likely the non-draft model).
3. Tame the whole-body lean (e.g. waist weight ~3-5, or a penalty) and test whether delta-action BC still reaches 100 %.
4. Multimodal demonstrations (human teleoperation) before drawing conclusions about diffusion.
5. A hand-orientation action head and harder tasks: articulated dishwasher door and rack, washer to dryer transfer, deformable cloth, navigation.
